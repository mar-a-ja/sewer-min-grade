How a pipe is graded

Enter each branch of the subdivision as a precinct: dwellings and net site area. Equivalent population per dwelling comes from the density, Table IW.1. Tick the branches that drain to the pipe you are designing. The tool adds their area and their population, then applies the peaking factor once.

Peak dry-weather flow is 5.83 times the average dry-weather flow, divided by total EP to the power 0.1. Average flow is 180 litres per EP per day. Groundwater is 0.01875 litres per second per hectare times the portion of the network below the water table (default 0.75). Rainfall inflow is 0.028 times the effective area times 1.2 times the rainfall intensity. Intensity is the Bureau of Meteorology one-hour depth at 39.35% annual exceedance probability, times (40 / area) to the power 0.12, times 1.5. The 21.4 mm/h default is the original spreadsheet figure; replace it with the Bureau figure for the site.

The wet-weather sum of those three is the design flow. The pipe must carry it without surcharge. The minimum grade is not based on that wet-weather flow. It is based on Qdmp, three quarters of peak dry-weather flow plus two thirds of any pumped inflow.

At that flow the hydraulic radius Rp fixes both grades: self-cleansing is 0.0135 / Rp, and sulphide slime control is 0.0338 / Rp, both in percent. Above DN150 the grade must be steeper than both, and also steeper than 80 divided by the internal diameter in millimetres. DN150 uses a table by the number of houses, from 1.25% for one house down to 0.70% at 35 houses. The maximum grade is the slope at which the dry-weather peak runs at 3 metres per second.

If you already know the depth, both grades are direct: radius from the depth, then the constant divided by that radius. The tool only iterates when the flow is known and the depth is not. Solve for Grade, Flow or Diameter at the top of the calculator. Flow asks what catchment would make your grade exactly the slime-control grade. Diameter asks for the smallest pipe whose minimum grade is no steeper than the grade you can build, and which can still carry the design flow.
